Weekly deficit of roughly 2,600 kcal against maintenance. That is the 0.3–0.4 kg per week pace, slow on purpose so you lose fat instead of muscle.

Five rules that make or break this

  1. Coconut oil: 4 tsp a day, total. Measure it into the pan. Biggest hidden calorie source in Kerala cooking.
  2. Grated coconut: 2 tbsp a day, total. Avial and thoran draw from the same budget.
  3. Measure rice once. Learn what 1 cup cooked looks like in your bowl, then you never measure again.
  4. Protein anchor goes on the plate first. Rice fills whatever space is left.
  5. Mayo and creamy sides on the side, never poured over. 2 tbsp of mayo is 200 kcal of pure fat.

Nuts share the 25 g seed budget, they don't add to it. Pick one nut per day at 15–20 g instead of that day's seeds. One casual handful is ~40 g, which is 230 kcal and 20 g of fat — a third of your daily fat allowance.
Dates go pre-workout, not as a snack. Near-zero fat and fast carbs, so 2 dates alongside the banana fuel training without touching your fat budget. Cap at 2–3; dates are 75 g carbs per 100 g. Ranking for your goals: peanuts, then pistachio and almonds for fiber, cashews last. Buy raw or dry-roasted, unsalted.

Fruit: 2 servings daily, one being the pre-workout banana. For the second prefer guava (2.6 g protein, 5.4 g fiber, best fruit here for you), papaya, orange or apple. Go easy on nendran banana, mango and jackfruit.

Omega-3: since you don't eat fish, flax and walnuts give ALA, but humans convert that to EPA/DHA poorly. An algae or fish oil capsule at 1–2 g EPA+DHA daily is a sensible gap-filler when you're ready. Raise it with your doctor alongside baseline bloodwork.

Daily skeleton

Five slots, same shape every day, rotating protein anchor. The repetition is deliberate — it makes the week shoppable and survivable on a workday.
Right now you train late and eat breakfast at 12, then lunch in the evening. That compresses everything. Shift the gym earlier so breakfast lands around 9:30, which spreads protein across the day and helps muscle gain.

Prep that makes this work

  1. Cook 750 g of chicken on Sunday and Wednesday. Portion and refrigerate.
  2. Keep 8 boiled eggs in the fridge at all times. Fastest 20 g of protein available.
  3. Soak soya chunks in the morning, ready by evening.
  4. Roasted chana in a box at your desk. This is the snack that replaces biscuits.
  5. Grind flax seeds in bulk, store in the fridge in an airtight jar.

What to track, in order of usefulness

  1. Waist at the navel, every Sunday morning. Single best marker you have.
  2. Photos, same light and position, every 2 weeks.
  3. Strength log. Waist shrinking while lifts go up is exactly what recomposition looks like.
  4. Weight as a weekly average, never a single day.
  5. Bioimpedance last. The two-rod machine swings with hydration and time of day. Trends only.
